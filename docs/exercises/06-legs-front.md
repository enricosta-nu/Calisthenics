# Batch 6 · Legs, front (quadricipiti)

Movement: bending and straightening the knee under bodyweight (squats and lunges). Main muscle:
**quadriceps**, helped by the **glutes**; single-leg versions also work the **gluteus medius**
(side of the hip) for balance.

## What differs between them

- **Two legs vs one leg:** the two-leg squat works the quadriceps more; single-leg squats work the gluteus medius and hamstrings more (source A). So rotating them trains the leg differently, not just harder.
- **Iso + dynamic:** the wall sit is the isometric partner for squats and lunges. Quadriceps work hardest at a 90° knee angle (source B).
- **Knee-only vs knee + hip:** the sissy squat keeps the hips straight, so it loads the quadriceps (including the rectus femoris) with little glute (source E). Most other rows share the work with the glutes.

## Sources

- **A · study.** McCurdy et al. (2010), *Comparison of lower extremity EMG between the 2-leg squat and modified single-leg squat in female athletes*, J Sport Rehabil 19(1):57–70. The single-leg (rear foot elevated) squat produced more gluteus medius and hamstring activity; the two-leg squat more quadriceps. [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/41969369_Comparison_of_Lower_Extremity_EMG_Between_the_2-Leg_Squat_and_Modified_Single-Leg_Squat_in_Female_Athletes)
- **B · study.** *Muscle activation differs between three different knee joint-angle positions during a maximal isometric back squat exercise*: 90° knee angle gave the highest quadriceps and gluteus maximus activity. [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4967668/)
- **C · study.** Ekstrom et al. (2007), JOSPT: EMG of trunk, hip and thigh muscles during exercises including the standing lunge. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18560185/)
- **D · reference.** NASM exercise library, *Squat jump*: quadriceps and glutes drive the jump; calves finish it and absorb the landing; land soft with knees in line with toes. [NASM](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/squat-jump), [StrengthLog](https://www.strengthlog.com/jump-squat/)
- **E · coaching.** Sissy squat: knee bending with the hips locked, so all four quadriceps muscles work, including the rectus femoris. [Garage Gym Reviews](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/sissy-squat), [BarBend](https://barbend.com/sissy-squat/)
- **F · study.** Single-leg squat studies: high gluteus maximus and medius activity (e.g. 64% and 82% of maximum) and high quadriceps activity. [JOSPT, gluteal activation during therapeutic exercises](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2009.2796), [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3201064/)

**Limits:** the pistol squat itself hasn't been studied much; source F measures single-leg
squats, which are close but not identical. The sissy squat relies on coaching sources.
