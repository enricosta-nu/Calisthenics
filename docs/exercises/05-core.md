# Batch 5 · Core (addominali)

Movements: keeping the body rigid (not arching, not bending sideways), and lifting the legs
towards the chest. Main muscles: **rectus abdominis** (the "six-pack"), **obliques** and the
deep **transversus abdominis**. Most leg-lifting exercises also work the **hip flexors**
(iliopsoas, rectus femoris) a lot.

## What differs between them

- **Resisting arching (plank, hollow) vs resisting sideways bending (side plank) vs lifting the legs (leg raises, L-sit):** three different jobs for the core. The app should rotate between them, not just repeat "core".
- **Leg raises are more hip flexor than abs.** A study with ultrasound found the abs change thickness less during leg raises than crunches (source D). Hanging knee raises were among the highest abdominal activators in another study (source A). Worth knowing for the Core focus.
- **Iso + dynamic:** hollow hold / L-sit / plank (iso) pair with leg raises / dragon flag (dynamic).

## Sources

- **A · study.** Escamilla et al. (2006), *Electromyographic analysis of traditional and nontraditional abdominal exercises*, Physical Therapy 86(5). Upper and lower rectus abdominis, internal oblique and lats highest for the ab wheel (pike, knee-up, roll-out), hanging knee-up with straps and reverse crunch; external oblique highest for the ab wheel and hanging knee-up. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16649890/)
- **B · study.** Ekstrom et al. (2007), *Electromyographic analysis of core trunk, hip, and thigh muscles during 9 rehabilitation exercises*, JOSPT. Side bridge: good for gluteus medius and external oblique; prone bridge (plank) below 45% of maximum, so better for endurance and stability. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18560185/)
- **C · coaching.** Hollow hold: rectus abdominis and transversus abdominis, with obliques and hip flexors. Dragon flag: rectus abdominis, obliques and transversus; hip flexors, shoulders, triceps and lats hold the position; learn the hollow first. [Hinge Health](https://www.hingehealth.com/resources/articles/hollow-body-hold/), [Calisteniapp](https://calisteniapp.com/articles/dragon-flag-exercises)
- **D · study + coaching.** Lying leg raise: mainly hip flexors, with the rectus abdominis holding the pelvis. Ultrasound study: abdominal thickness change during a crunch was 36% greater than during a leg raise. [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10824285/), [NFPT](https://www.nfpt.com/blog/leg-raise-not-ab-movement)
- **E · coaching.** L-sit: hip flexors and rectus abdominis hold the legs; lats, triceps and front deltoids press the body up; quadriceps lock the knees. [GMB](https://gmb.io/l-sit/), [BarBend](https://barbend.com/l-sit/)

**Limits:** no muscle-activity studies for the hollow hold, L-sit or dragon flag; those rows rely on coaching sources.
