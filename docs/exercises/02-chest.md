# Batch 2 · Chest (pettorali)

**Status: approved; rows 8–11 added at your request, to check.**

Movement: pushing the body away from the floor or bars, arms in front. Main muscle:
**pectoralis major**, helped by the **triceps** (straightening the elbow) and the **front
deltoid**. The serratus anterior and the abs keep the shoulder blades and the body in line.

The band helps most at the bottom of the dip, where it's stretched. Move to thinner bands over time.

## What differs between them

- **Easier to harder by body angle:** hands up (incline) → floor → feet up (decline). A study measured how much of your bodyweight you push in each: about 41–55% hands elevated, 64% on the floor, 70–74% feet elevated (source B). The app can use this to scale the same exercise to the user's level.
- **Hand width:** narrow hands work the chest *and* triceps more than wide hands (source A). Narrow ones (diamond push-ups) are in the Arms batch.
- **Dip torso angle:** leaning forward shifts the dip towards the chest; staying upright shifts it to the triceps (source L). So the forward-lean dip is here and the upright dip is in Arms.
- **Rings vs floor:** ring push-ups raised pectoralis major activity compared with the floor in one study; the extra effort goes mostly to the stabilising muscles (source J). Ring dips beat bar dips for several muscles (source G).
- **Power:** the clap push-up had the highest loading and push-off rates among plyometric push-ups (source I). Good for a finisher, not for long sets.
- **Iso + dynamic:** the bottom hold is the isometric partner for push-ups (your rule 1).

## Sources

**study** = peer-reviewed research that measured muscle activity or force; **reference** = an
established organisation or fitness test; **coaching** = coaching websites (technique, weaker
as evidence).

- **A · study.** Cogley et al. (2005), *Comparison of muscle activation using various hand positions during the push-up exercise*, J Strength Cond Res 19(3):628–633. Pectoralis major and triceps more active with a narrow base than a wide base. [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7665817_Comparison_of_Muscle_Activation_Using_Various_Hand_Positions_During_the_Push-Up_Exercise)
- **B · study.** Ebben et al. (2011), *Kinetic analysis of several variations of push-ups*, J Strength Cond Res. Share of bodyweight pushed: feet up 61 cm 74%, feet up 30 cm 70%, floor 64%, hands up 30 cm 55%, knees 49%, hands up 61 cm 41%. [JSCR](https://journals.lww.com/nsca-jscr/fulltext/2011/10000/kinetic_analysis_of_several_variations_of_push_ups.31.aspx)
- **C · study.** Muscle-activity studies of push-ups: front deltoid, triceps, trapezius and pectoralis major most involved. [PubMed review of push-up variations](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35599715/), [Suspension vs traditional push-ups, PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3916913/)
- **D · study.** Parallel-bar dips at elbow angles of 75°, 85° and 95°: pectoralis major similar at all angles; triceps highest at 75°. [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/357635252_EFFECT_OF_ELBOW_ANGLE_ON_TRICEPS_BRACHII_AND_PECTORALIS_MAJOR_MUSCLE_ACTIVITY_DURING_PARALLEL_BAR_DIP)
- **E · reference.** NASM exercise library, *Archer push-up*: pectoralis major and triceps of the working arm; front deltoid, serratus anterior and core secondary. [NASM](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/archer-push-up)
- **F · coaching.** Push-up bottom hold: chest, triceps, front deltoids and core. [Topend Sports](https://www.topendsports.com/testing/tests/push-up-isometric-hold.htm), [The Movement Athlete](https://themovementathlete.com/pushup-hold-2cm-off-the-floor/)
- **G · study.** McKenzie et al. (2022), *Bench, bar, and ring dips: do kinematics and muscle activity differ?*, IJERPH 19(20). Six muscles peaked higher on bar dips than bench dips; eight (pectoralis major, front deltoid, triceps, upper trapezius, serratus anterior, infraspinatus, lats, biceps) higher on ring dips than bench dips. [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9603242/)
- **H · coaching.** Hindu push-up: flows from downward dog to a chest-forward swoop; chest, front shoulders and triceps, with core, lower back and lats; improves shoulder and upper-back mobility. [Calisthenics.com](https://calisthenics.com/exercise/hindu-push-ups/), [Men's Journal](https://www.mensjournal.com/fitness/the-centuries-old-hindu-pushup-that-undoes-the-damage-of-sitting-all-day)
- **I · study.** *Ground reaction force patterns in plyometric push-ups*, J Strength Cond Res (2011): the clap push-up had the highest loading and propulsion rates and shorter ground contact than box-drop push-ups. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21986698/)
- **J · study.** Ring and suspension push-ups: pectoralis major activity higher on rings than on the floor; suspended push-ups raise the stabilising muscles (triceps, trapezius) more. [Push-ups on different surfaces](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374806753_Electromyographic_Activities_of_Pectoralis_Major_and_Triceps_Brachii_Muscles_During_Push-Up_Exercises_on_Different_Surfaces), [JSSM, suspension systems](https://www.jssm.org/jssm-13-502.xml-Fulltext)
- **L · coaching.** Dip torso angle: upright = triceps, forward lean of 15–30° = chest. [StrongLifts](https://stronglifts.com/dips/), [PureGym](https://www.puregym.com/exercises/chest/dips/)

**Limits:** no muscle-activity studies found for the archer push-up, bottom hold or Hindu push-up; those rows rely on NASM and coaching sources. Source L is coaching; I found no study measuring dip torso angle directly.
