## Rules agreed so far

- **Exercises at the bar are at least level 2.**
- **Bands only for beginners:** a band is passive help for users who can't yet do the basic
  version. It's allowed on pull-ups, chin-ups, Australian rows and dips, and never on advanced
  or skill exercises, which have their own easier steps instead.

## About resistance bands

**The rule:** bands are for the very beginning, when strength isn't enough yet (beginners, or
anyone who can't do a first pull-up or row). Then they're phased out.

**Why that fits the evidence:** a band helps most where it's stretched furthest, at the bottom
of the pull (the dead hang), which is exactly where a beginner fails. As you rise, the help fades
([Torokhtiy](https://torokhtiy.com/blogs/guides/assisted-pull-ups-with-resistance-bands)). NASM
lists the band-assisted pull-up as a standard beginner progression
([NASM](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/band-assisted-pull-up)).

**How the app should use them:**

1. Offer the band version only to users who can't yet do the exercise.
2. Pair it with negatives (lowering-only reps), which train the full range.
3. Suggest thinner bands over the weeks, then the unassisted exercise.

One coaching site reports a 2019 study where a band-assisted group improved *least* at
unassisted pull-ups and a negatives-only group improved *most*
([BullBarFit](https://bullbarfit.com/blogs/updates/the-band-aid-solution-why-your-resistance-band-isnt-teaching-you-pull-ups-and-how-to-fix-it)).
I couldn't find the study itself, so treat it as unverified, but it's another reason to keep
bands short-term.
