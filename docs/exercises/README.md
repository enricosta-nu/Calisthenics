## About resistance bands

A band gives the most help where it's stretched the most, which is usually the bottom of the
movement (the dead hang in a pull-up, the lowest point of a dip or Nordic curl). As you rise
the help fades. So bands are good for doing more reps with good form and for learning the
movement, but they don't fully train the hardest position. The safest way to use them:

1. **Combine bands with negatives** (lowering-only reps), which train the whole range.
2. **Move to thinner bands** over the weeks, then to the unassisted exercise.

On the evidence: NASM describes the band-assisted pull-up as a standard progression
([NASM](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/band-assisted-pull-up)). Coaching
sources describe the "most help at the bottom" effect
([Torokhtiy](https://torokhtiy.com/blogs/guides/assisted-pull-ups-with-resistance-bands)). One
coaching site reports a 2019 study where a band-assisted group improved *least* at unassisted
pull-ups and a negatives-only group improved *most*
([BullBarFit](https://bullbarfit.com/blogs/updates/the-band-aid-solution-why-your-resistance-band-isnt-teaching-you-pull-ups-and-how-to-fix-it)).
I couldn't find that study itself, so treat it as unverified, but it's consistent with advice 1.
