# Batch 1 · Back (dorsali) · vertical pull

**Status: approved.**

Movement: pulling the body up to a bar overhead. Main muscle: **latissimus dorsi** (the big back
muscle), helped by the elbow flexors (biceps, brachialis, brachioradialis) and the muscles that
move the shoulder blades (lower and middle trapezius, rhomboids).

## What differs between them (so the app can rotate without boring the user)

- **Pull-up vs chin-up:** the same movement with a different grip. Chin-ups (palms toward you) use more **biceps and chest**. Pull-ups (palms away) use more **lower trapezius** (source A). They're good swaps for each other.
- **Holds vs reps:** the pull-up hold and the dead hang are isometric. By your rule, they can sit next to a dynamic pull (pull-up hold → pull-up is fine).
- **Easier options for beginners:** scapular pull-ups teach the first centimetres of the pull-up (source A found pull-ups start with the lower trapezius pulling the shoulder blades down). Negatives build strength for the full pull-up, because you're stronger lowering than lifting (source G).
- **Harder option:** the archer pull-up loads one arm at a time, a step towards the one-arm pull-up.

## Sources

How much weight each one carries: **study** = peer-reviewed research that measured muscle activity;
**reference** = a well-known exercise database; **coaching** = coaching websites (useful for
technique, weaker as evidence).

- **A · study.** Youdas et al. (2010), *Surface electromyographic activation patterns and elbow joint motion during a pull-up, chin-up, or Perfect-Pullup rotational exercise*, J Strength Cond Res 24(12). Measured: latissimus dorsi 117–130% of maximum, biceps 78–96%, infraspinatus 71–79%, lower trapezius 45–56%, pectoralis major 44–57%. Chin-up: more pectoralis major and biceps; pull-up: more lower trapezius. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21068680/)
- **B · reference.** ExRx.net, *Pull-up*: target latissimus dorsi; synergists brachialis, brachioradialis, biceps, teres major, rear deltoid, infraspinatus, teres minor, rhomboids, levator scapulae, lower & middle trapezius, pectoralis minor. [exrx.net](https://exrx.net/WeightExercises/LatissimusDorsi/BWPullup)
- **C · study.** Dickie et al. (2017), *Electromyographic analysis of muscle activation during pull-up variations*, J Electromyogr Kinesiol 32:30–36. Compared underhand, overhand, neutral and rope grips; middle trapezius higher with overhand than neutral grip. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1050641116302978)
- **D · reference.** Flexed-arm hang test (used in school and US Marine fitness tests): elbow flexors and shoulder extensors (latissimus dorsi, rear deltoid) as main muscles, with grip, core and lower trapezius secondary. [Topend Sports](https://www.topendsports.com/testing/tests/flexed-arm-hang.htm)
- **E · coaching.** Scapular pull-up: straight arms, lift the body by pulling the shoulder blades down; lower trapezius and latissimus. [TrainingBeta](https://www.trainingbeta.com/the-scapular-pull-up/), [Hevy](https://www.hevyapp.com/exercises/scapular-pull-ups/)
- **F · coaching.** Negative pull-up: lats and biceps, 3–5 second descent, 3 sets of 3–5 for beginners. [Inspire US Foundation](https://www.inspireusafoundation.org/negative-eccentric-pull-ups/), [Calixpert](https://www.calixpert.com/exercises/negative-pull-up)
- **G · study.** 5-week eccentric, isometric and plyometric pull-up training in climbers: maximum strength improved in all three groups. [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10813506/)
- **H · coaching.** Archer pull-up as a step towards the one-arm pull-up: lats and biceps of the pulling arm, core and grip. [GORNATION](https://www.gornation.com/blogs/calisthenics-exercises/archer-pull-up), [50pullups](https://www.50pullups.com/archer-pull-ups)
- **I · coaching.** Dead hang: grip / forearm flexors work hardest; lats stretched under load; also decompresses the spine. [Fitness Volt](https://fitnessvolt.com/dead-hangs/), [Calixpert](https://www.calixpert.com/exercises/dead-hang)

**Limits:** I could only read ExRx through search results (its site is blocked from this
environment), and I found no muscle-activity studies for archer pull-ups, scapular pull-ups or
dead hangs, so those rows rely on coaching sources.
