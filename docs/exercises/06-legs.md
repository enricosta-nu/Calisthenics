# Batch 6 · Legs (gambe)

**Status: to review.** Batches 6 and 7 merged at your request: legs aren't the core of
calisthenics, so 10 exercises in total, 5 for the front of the legs and 5 for the back.

- **Front of the legs:** bending and straightening the knee (squats). Main muscles:
  **quadriceps**, with the **glutes**; single-leg versions also work the **gluteus medius** (side
  of the hip) for balance.
- **Back of the legs:** extending the hip (bridges, hinges) and resisting knee bending (Nordic
  curl). Main muscles: **gluteus maximus** and **hamstrings**. Your 12 trainings have almost none
  of this, so these rows add something new rather than more variety.

## Easier versions (no bands)

Bands aren't used for legs. The easier steps are:

| # | Exercise | Easier version |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Pistol squat | Hold a post or door frame; or squat down to a box and stand up on one leg |
| 10 | Nordic hamstring curl | Lowering only, as slowly as possible, catching yourself with the hands; then a shorter range (sources H, I) |

## What differs between them

- **Two legs vs one leg:** the two-leg squat works the quadriceps more; single-leg squats work the gluteus medius and hamstrings more (source A). So rotating them trains the leg differently, not just harder.
- **Front vs back:** squats are mostly quadriceps. Bridges, single-leg RDLs and Nordic curls balance them with glutes and hamstrings.
- **Iso + dynamic:** the wall sit (quadriceps work hardest at a 90° knee angle, source B) and the glute bridge hold are the isometric partners.
- **Nordic curl:** very hard, but adding it to training programmes roughly halved hamstring injuries across 8,459 athletes (source H). Start with a few slow lowering-only reps.

## Sources

- **A · study.** McCurdy et al. (2010), *Comparison of lower extremity EMG between the 2-leg squat and modified single-leg squat in female athletes*, J Sport Rehabil 19(1):57–70. Single-leg (rear foot elevated) squat: more gluteus medius and hamstrings; two-leg squat: more quadriceps. [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/41969369_Comparison_of_Lower_Extremity_EMG_Between_the_2-Leg_Squat_and_Modified_Single-Leg_Squat_in_Female_Athletes)
- **B · study.** *Muscle activation differs between three different knee joint-angle positions during a maximal isometric back squat exercise*: 90° gave the highest quadriceps and gluteus maximus activity. [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4967668/)
- **C · reference.** NASM exercise library, *Squat jump*: quadriceps and glutes drive the jump; calves finish it and absorb the landing; land soft with knees in line with toes. [NASM](https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/squat-jump)
- **D · study.** Single-leg squat studies: high gluteus maximus and medius activity (e.g. 64% and 82% of maximum) and high quadriceps activity. [JOSPT](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2009.2796), [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3201064/)
- **E · study.** Ekstrom et al. (2007), JOSPT: bridge and unilateral bridge among 9 exercises measured for glute and hamstring activity. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18560185/)
- **F · reference + coaching.** Lateral lunge: gluteus maximus, quadriceps and adductors main movers; gluteus medius when stepping out. [ISSA](https://www.issaonline.com/blogs/training-tips/how-to-do-side-lunges-work-your-quads-hamstrings-glutes)
- **G · study.** Single-leg Romanian deadlift with dumbbell and flywheel (2023): gluteus maximus, gluteus medius, biceps femoris, erector spinae and adductors measured. [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10716453/)
- **H · study.** van Dyk, Behan & Whiteley (2019), *Including the Nordic hamstring exercise in injury prevention programmes halves the rate of hamstring injuries*, Br J Sports Med 53(21):1362–1370. [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331367089_Including_the_Nordic_hamstring_exercise_in_injury_prevention_programmes_halves_the_rate_of_hamstring_injuries_A_systematic_review_and_meta-analysis_of_8459_athletes)
- **I · coaching.** Band-assisted Nordic curl: band anchored behind, looped under the chest; progress to thinner bands, then lowering-only, then full reps. [Muscle & Strength](https://www.muscleandstrength.com/exercises/band-assisted-nordic-hamstring-curl), [Mirafit](https://mirafit.co.uk/blog/nordic-curl-progression-and-regression/)

**Limits:** source G used weights, not bodyweight. The pistol squat itself is little studied;
source D measures single-leg squats, which are close. I found no source for the band-assisted
pistol squat; it's common practice, but check it with your coach.
